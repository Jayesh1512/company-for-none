# Expert Agent Marketplace — hackathon build plan

Working title: Near Council · Planning baseline: 10 November 2026  
Product source: `PRD.md`, preserved unchanged

## 1. Recommended outcome

Demonstrate one business promise: **a company purchases an expert-designed positioning review, watches agents improve the recommendation through evidence and challenge, and approves the result for company knowledge.**

Agent-to-agent communication is the centerpiece. The marketplace supplies a defined service and contributed judgment; NEAR Intents is the priority payment dependency. Bonfire and NEAR AI are proposed dependencies for knowledge and agent work. Their capabilities have not been verified in this planning task.

The original project inspection found the PRD, a short README, and an icon, with no product implementation. The subsequently available `BUILD_PLAN_CRITIQUE.md` and `BUILD_PLAN_REVISED.md` are preserved as separate planning references. Paperclip is inspiration; reuse is not assumed. This document selects no technology and begins no implementation.

The hackathon milestone is a convincing, honestly labeled demonstration. Full commercial readiness and repeat-purchase validation follow after the event; they are not prerequisites for starting the demo build.

## 2. Explicit assumptions

- **Event:** deadline, judging criteria, submission rules, required vendor use, and demo length are unknown. Verify them first. Use a three-minute presentation until the actual limit is known.
- **Team:** assume two builders and one person handling product, example content, and pitch; roles may overlap. Use one short event window, without promising a number of days. A solo team cuts scope rather than compressing the same work.
- **Buyer:** provisionally, a small B2B software company deciding how to position one product launch. This is a demo choice, not a validated initial market.
- **Demo scope:** one organization, two prepared human members, three specialized agents, one listing, and one assignment. Membership administration can wait.
- **Evidence:** three short fictional or consented sources: product facts, customer notes, and the current message. Include a meaningful contradiction.
- **Expert:** seek one original contribution and permission to use it. If unavailable, label an organizer-authored sample “Illustrative guidance; no external expert contribution.” Never imply celebrity authorization.
- **Offer:** provisionally, a fixed-price review, one preference revision, and profile use within this service. These boundaries simplify the demo; they do not settle future profile licensing, recurring use, service categories, or the wider expert marketplace. Validate those choices after the event. Standalone profile purchasing and paid human review are outside the demo.
- **Payments:** select an acceptable demonstration amount only after verifying the supported NEAR Intents route. No currency, fee, testing environment, refund, or payout capability is assumed.

## 3. One service and the smallest complete journey

The positioning review delivers a diagnosis, two defensible directions, one evidence-based recommendation, a revised positioning statement, and three practical experiments. It identifies assumptions and dissent. If evidence cannot support a promised element, explain the gap rather than invent support; resolve the scope before selling that package. Correct unsupported claims, wrong sources, or missing promised content without consuming the one preference revision.

The `SOUL.md` supplies evaluation principles and trade-off rules. Show a rule changing a recommendation; a portrait or recognizable voice is insufficient. For example, preferring the segment with evidence of urgent need over the largest hypothetical audience is an illustrative rule until actually contributed by an expert. Real paid value and willingness to pay remain hypotheses.

The journey:

1. Open the prepared company: goal, owner, colleague, sources, and budget.
2. Inspect the evidence analyst, positioning lead, and critical reviewer. Their curated skills state inputs, outputs, and limits; profiles show human owners, permitted knowledge, and work allowances.
3. Select the service. Understand its contributor, authorization status, deliverable, price, revision boundary, and actual human involvement.
4. Approve as owner within budget; complete verified payment or explicitly disclosed simulation. Unconfirmed live payment cannot unlock paid work.
5. Watch evidence pass to the lead, the reviewer challenge its actual recommendation, and the lead revise or explain disagreement.
6. Inspect the package, request a focused revision or accept, then approve saving it as trusted company knowledge.

Agent-written conclusions always remain agent-generated. Authorized guidance does not make them the expert's personal opinion. Attribute only genuine human comments to the actual contributor.

## 4. Scope: live, prepared, simulated, and deferred

**Must work live:** visible company context; service selection; owner approval and enforced spending cap; three agents responding to the current brief and each other's work; source-supported delivery; a bounded discussion with stop control; one revision; human approval before saving knowledge. Repeated approval must not buy twice. These are demo controls, not proof of production identity or access security.

**May be prepared:** company and two members, sources, skill registry, agent profiles, one listing, and budget. Expert onboarding can be manual: record who supplied the principles and authorized use. Disclose donated or paid participation without implying continuing supply or viable compensation. A saved prior run and recording may support the pitch when labeled. Second-organization switching is optional; seeded switching does not prove isolation.

**May be simulated with visible disclosure:** payment confirmation/failure if the real route is unavailable; a company-knowledge experience if Bonfire is unavailable; an illustrative human-review comment. Say “simulated purchase; no money moved,” “demo knowledge workspace,” or “fictional review” as appropriate. Never call these working vendor connections. Claim NEAR AI support only after actual agent work through it is demonstrated. A recorded discussion is not live collaboration.

**Defer:** invitations and full membership administration, reliable cross-organization controls, broad knowledge management, open expert publishing, many listings, advanced discovery, independent profile licensing, human-review scheduling, expert payouts, complete refund operations, subscriptions, unrestricted agents, and delegated purchasing.

Do not open real-money public sales or accept confidential inputs on demo controls. Use fictional material or material explicitly cleared for every intended viewer, agent service, and submission artifact; ordinary research consent alone is insufficient. Any real payment proof uses a small, team-funded demonstration with a named payer, recipient, all-in cap, and owner for unresolved funds. This is not a public sale or willingness-to-pay evidence. Broader PRD requirements remain the product destination. Section 8 covers readiness before a small paid pilot.

## 5. Ordered delivery phases

Effort sizes: **S** is a bounded task, **M** connects several steps, **L** is a complete or uncertain workflow. Suggested percentages allocate available team effort, not elapsed days. Vendor waiting time is separate. Protect the last 20% for rehearsal and submission. Before work, map each owner below to an actual teammate and a share of available effort; overlapping roles cannot spend the same capacity twice. A solo team starts with the cut list in Section 7. Responsibility labels are not extra hires.

### Phase 0 — Freeze the story and prove the risky dependencies

**Effort:** S–M, 10%. **Owner:** product lead. **Dependencies:** rules, accounts, PRD.

Tasks:

1. Check event requirements and choose the one buyer question and decision package.
2. Choose the evidence and secure permission for real names/materials.
3. Assign the early dependency proofs below and start them now, before the main collaboration build. Record observed behavior, not assumed features.
4. For each event-required connection, record the actual eligibility requirement and required evidence. Apply the same owner, evidence, and cutoff rule to any additional mandatory dependency; renegotiate scope if it cannot fit.
5. Record an explicit pass/change/fallback decision by the end of Phase 1, before the remaining 70% of planned effort. Any retry must fit inside that cutoff.

Early proof assignments:

- **NEAR Intents — purchase journey lead:** show the intended approval/payment steps, all-in amount, and a matching confirmed outcome from the real service in the verified demonstration mode. Keep its reference and dated evidence; distinguish real funds from a supported non-production demonstration. Establish how pending/uncertain outcomes are recognized and owned. If unavailable at the cutoff, disclose simulation with no money moved; mark any unmet payment-integration requirement as a submission blocker.
- **NEAR AI — agent experience lead:** obtain an actual response to a small current brief through NEAR AI and retain evidence of that use. The complete three-agent exchange is checked in Phase 2. If unavailable at the cutoff, declare NEAR AI support unproved; any alternative live agent work must be named as such and cannot satisfy a NEAR AI requirement.
- **Bonfire — workspace/offer lead:** show one approved source used and one human-approved result saved and reopened in Bonfire, with dated evidence. If unavailable at the cutoff, label the substitute a demo knowledge workspace; it cannot satisfy a Bonfire requirement.

**Milestone:** frozen demo promise and vendor proof list.

**Acceptance:** the team explains the paid outcome, expert contribution, collaboration purpose, and which connections are demonstrated. Missing event-required proof is an explicit submission risk.

**Done:** name the proof owners, applicable requirements, evidence to collect, and cutoff decisions. Unverified capabilities remain labeled dependencies; mock behavior does not satisfy a real-integration requirement.

### Phase 1 — Prepare the company and purchasable offer

**Effort:** M, 20%. **Owner:** workspace/offer lead. **Dependencies:** Phase 0 scope and permitted evidence.

Tasks:

1. Prepare the organization, two members, goal, sources, and budget.
2. Prepare the authorized profile or labeled illustrative alternative; show its judgment principles.
3. Create three agent profiles and skill entries with owners, inputs, outputs, source permissions, and limits.
4. Complete the listing: suitability, contribution, deliverable, example, price, revision, and human-participation label.
5. Make owner approval and budget checks work. Block the member and an over-budget request; prevent duplicate approval.
6. At the cutoff, choose pass, change, or disclosed fallback for every dependency using the Phase 0 proof criteria. Close further feasibility attempts; Phase 3 completes only the proved route or declared fallback. Cut optional scope first to protect the early NEAR Intents attempt. Missing mandatory proof means change to an eligible submission path if permitted or report the blocker.

**Milestone:** the prepared company can select and approve a comprehensible service.

**Acceptance:** a teammate identifies what is bought, who contributed, what it costs, and who can approve without narration. Permission and budget checks behave as stated.

**Done:** brief and offer are ready for the agents; every missing connection has a concrete degraded-demo choice.

### Phase 2 — Make collaboration change the result

**Effort:** L, 30%. **Owner:** agent experience lead. **Dependencies:** Phase 1 inputs and usable agent-work route.

Tasks:

1. Have the analyst hand evidence, gaps, and uncertainty to the lead.
2. Have the lead apply profile principles to options and a recommendation, referencing permitted sources.
3. Pass the actual recommendation to the reviewer, then its challenge back to the lead. Require revision, reasoned rejection, or visible dissent; do not script theatrical disagreement.
4. Bound work to one challenge/response round and synthesis. Show handoffs, questions, progress, and a working stop action. Additional paid work requires approval.
5. Produce the package, support one focused revision, and require human approval before trusted-knowledge saving.
6. Repeat with a changed source or question to show the run responds to current inputs.

**Milestone:** live collaboration produces a useful, inspectable decision package.

**Acceptance:** a teammate traces a recommendation to evidence and guidance, identifies the reviewer's effect or reasoned rejection, and distinguishes assumptions. Stopping prevents further work. Changed inputs can change the result.

**Done:** two genuine agent runs, including a changed brief, complete; record their agent-run times. Include at least one source-supported changed recommendation; do not force disagreement on every run. This establishes a working collaboration experience, not superiority over a single agent.

### Phase 3 — Complete purchase through approved delivery

**Effort:** M–L, 20%. **Owner:** purchase journey lead. **Dependencies:** Phases 1–2 and vendor decisions.

Tasks:

1. Complete the proved NEAR Intents route or declared simulation. Label the mode throughout. For live payment, show actual pending/confirmed outcomes and start the matching assignment once after confirmation.
2. Keep the approved amount held while payment is pending or uncertain. Return to the same purchase after interruption; do not invite a second payment or release the hold until the first outcome is resolved. Stopping work does not cancel money movement. Late confirmation after stop or cancellation does not restart work: the purchase lead reconciles it and obtains explicit continuation approval or owns the remedy.
3. Check repeated approval, an uncertain outcome, and a late confirmation without another purchase or unapproved work. Preserve the brief and cap. Simulated adverse cases must be labeled as such; they are not observed vendor behavior. In simulation, approval/cap checks still work, but no funds, settlement, refund, or payout operation is claimed.
4. Connect purchase, discussion, deliverable, revision, and approved knowledge. Unapproved output stays a proposal.
5. Carry the proved Bonfire/NEAR AI use into the journey; put disclosures beside substitutions and in submission materials. Finish before the protected rehearsal allocation. Do not reopen failed dependency experiments or claim eligibility from incomplete proof.

**Milestone:** one continuous journey with an accurate payment claim.

**Acceptance:** one approval buys one assignment; unconfirmed live payment does not; approval/cap controls hold; saving needs human approval. Every claimed integration has demonstrable evidence.

**Done:** full rehearsal plus over-budget and interrupted-purchase checks pass; record approval-to-payment and total journey times. Payment mode is explicitly real, a verified supported non-production demonstration, or simulation. Simulation remains incomplete NEAR Intents proof.

### Phase 4 — Rehearse and submit

**Effort:** M, protected 20%. **Owner:** pitch lead. **Dependencies:** Phase 3 or declared fallback.

Tasks:

1. Rehearse twice within the actual presentation limit using the slower measured run plus a visible time buffer. Fix blockers and misleading claims only. If the complete live route will not fit, use the labeled prior-run presentation below; do not assume payment or generation finishes within the script.
2. Prepare a genuine-run recording, saved package, integration evidence, and limitation list.
3. Ask an outsider what the company bought, what the expert added, and what changed through collaboration. Correct confusing presentation.
4. Submit required materials with working/incomplete features and the next validation step.

**Milestone:** concise, honest business-outcome demo.

**Acceptance:** pitch shows consequential agent communication, useful output, spending control, and accurate live/prepared/simulated labels.

**Done:** submission, live path, backup, and list of unproven claims are complete. A demo does not establish paid demand or production readiness.

## 6. Three-minute judge-facing script

Adjust to actual event rules; these are presentation timings, not build estimates. Use a fully live journey only if both rehearsals finish payment by 1:10 and the agent package by 2:15 with a buffer. Otherwise start the presentation with a clearly labeled completed prior run for the purchase-to-delivery story, and use a short live action only if measured to fit. A prior run preserves the presentation, not proof of live behavior or an event's live-demo eligibility. Actual pending payment must not be bypassed to start work early.

- **0:00–0:25:** introduce the company decision and three sources; disclose fictional/consented material.
- **0:25–0:50:** show the service, contribution, price, human involvement, and owner approval.
- **0:50–1:10:** show payment outcome/evidence or disclose simulation. Name working vendor connections.
- **1:10–2:15:** show evidence handoff, recommendation, substantive challenge, and response. Highlight a source or judgment rule changing the result. In the live route, start only after confirmed payment or explicitly simulated confirmation; in the prior-run route, keep the prior-run label visible.
- **2:15–2:45:** inspect the revised statement and experiments, follow evidence, approve knowledge saving, and briefly show an over-budget request blocked.
- **2:45–3:00:** name the largest incomplete piece and next test: paid value, expert contribution, and collaboration advantage.

## 7. Cut order and fallback

Cut extra listings, second-organization switching, human-review samples, decorative views, and secondary settings first. Preserve one service, three meaningful roles, evidence, deliverable, purchase approval, cap, and disclosure.

If NEAR Intents remains unavailable after the cutoffs, show a clearly simulated purchase without moving customer money. This cannot meet a rule requiring real payment integration. If Bonfire is unavailable, label the knowledge substitute. If a live run fails, retry once within the rehearsed allowance, then show the labeled recording and state the failure; a recording cannot pass the live-agent acceptance gate.

The major risks are generic output despite impressive discussion, dependency delays, excessive marketplace scope, and overstated participation/integrations. Protect the company-specific outcome, bounded work, early proof, and accurate claims.

## 8. After the event: small paid validation

**Readiness effort: L, reassessed from demo gaps; outside hackathon completion.** Before real customer money or confidential data, establish dependable membership/access separation, knowledge permissions, authorized guidance rights, payment/spending behavior, delivery terms, corrections/revisions, refund ownership, expert compensation, and support. Verify vendor suitability. Offer actual human review only with capacity and consent to share materials. Provider errors must be corrected without consuming the preference revision.

**Readiness done:** rehearse a real purchase, failure/recovery, delivery, revision, acceptance, approved knowledge, and an owned refund remedy. No critical access, rights, payment, or attribution gap remains. Disclosed manual operations are acceptable for this supported pilot.

**Pilot effort: M–L plus participant availability.** Recruit three companies with real decisions and one authorized expert. Freeze the three-company cohort, base price, scope, and readout date before offers; keep declined or failed purchases in the denominator. Record refusals, discounts, refunds, relationships, expert effort, and staff assistance. Include a multi-member company; offer separately priced human review only if genuinely available. Ask whether buyers expect reusable profile access or another service category; demo restrictions are still hypotheses.

For each consented brief, compare three outputs under the same evidence, deliverable scope, and maximum work allowance:

1. A competent ordinary single-agent review using public guidance.
2. A single-agent review with authorized expert guidance.
3. A collaborative review with that same expert guidance.

The first comparison tests the expert contribution; the second tests collaboration. Freeze inputs, exclude human editing before scoring, vary presentation order, conceal production method, and record actual cost/time. Log operator preparation separately, apply the same prepared inputs to all versions, and label assisted results; shared preparation is not evidence of expert or collaboration value. Buyers score evidence, relevance, trade-offs, and actionability; seek an independent qualified reviewer where possible and disclose contributor conflicts. Ties and disagreement remain inconclusive. Measure actual human-review value separately.

**Proposed continuation gate:** two of the original three complete the base purchase at the frozen price, receive useful delivery within the preference-revision scope, and retain the full charge with no unresolved material issue at readout. Report discounted, incentivized, partly refunded, and fully refunded orders separately; they do not satisfy that retained-price count. Corrections remain separate from preference revisions. Two prefer expert guidance over ordinary guidance and two prefer collaboration over expert-single, with concrete improvements; count ties as inconclusive. Delivery costs include assistance and expert time; no critical unresolved issue remains. These are directional investment gates, not statistical claims. Weak results trigger revision, not expansion; applause and stated interest are not paid demand.

Before enrollment, choose a fixed follow-up end date informed by customers' next real decision dates, and set the readout after each promised review/remedy window. Ask what recommendations were used and seek a genuine next purchase. At that date choose proceed/change/stop; do not extend automatically or count included corrections, split scope, or stated intent as repeat demand. No next decision means recurrence remains unproven, not passed.

**Pilot done:** a short readout covers paid outcomes, separate expert/collaboration effects, costs, help, negative findings, and expansion decision. Broader self-service launch and marketplace additions follow this evidence.
