# Near Council — revised product build plan

Planning date: 10 November 2026. Source requirements: `PRD.md`. This is a standalone proposal, informed by `BUILD_PLAN_CRITIQUE.md`. It preserves both original source documents. It authorizes no implementation, vendor choice, customer charge, or spending by itself.

## 1. Product promise, requirements, and proposed choices

Near Council brings a company's people and specialized agents together to solve business problems. Agent-to-agent communication is central: agents exchange evidence, develop recommendations, challenge assumptions, and show what changed or remains unresolved. People control access, spending, and business decisions.

The marketplace sells company-specific deliverables shaped by original, authorized expert judgment. Public information, a famous name, or personality imitation is insufficient. An expert's `SOUL.md` describes contributed principles, evaluation criteria, working style, and trade-offs. It does not grant new skills, company access, spending authority, or the expert's personal endorsement.

### Requirements carried from the PRD

- Multiple human members, organization creation and switching, clear active organization, membership and spending responsibilities, and separation of knowledge, work, purchases, and budgets.
- Agents with human owners, purpose, skills, working approach, permitted knowledge, and allowances; a curated skill registry describing inputs, deliverables, and limitations.
- Inspectable agent discussions, member-initiated alternative views, approved invitations, visible handoffs and disagreements, progress, and approve/redirect/pause/stop controls.
- Curated paid services with clear expert contributions, deliverables, examples, limits, total price, turnaround, revisions, and actual human involvement where offered.
- Bonfire for company knowledge, source references, controlled use, and human approval before agent output becomes trusted knowledge. Private knowledge is not marketplace content without explicit permission.
- NEAR AI supporting agent work and NEAR Intents supporting payments; budgets, approvals, and distinguishable payment outcomes.
- Deliverable review, an understandable end-to-end journey without routine assistance for release, and repeat paid demand before expanding the marketplace.

### Proposed pilot choices, not agreed requirements or validated findings

1. Start with a positioning review for small B2B software teams facing a real launch decision. Validate this against reachable customers and their existing alternatives; change the category before full build if evidence favors another narrow problem.
2. Sell one fixed-price service with one preference revision and an optional, separately priced actual expert review. The PRD supports fixed-price services; the exact category, scope, prices, and revision boundaries remain proposals.
3. Limit use of the authorized profile to the purchased service. Buyers retain permitted internal use of their deliverable; they do not receive the reusable profile, redistribution rights, or ongoing access to the expert. Confirm these boundaries with buyers, experts, and the user. Do not describe this as validation of standalone profile demand.
4. Use three preset agent roles and four curated skills; allow one active paid assignment per organization. Maintain bounded member-triggered challenge and invitation controls. Broader agent creation, councils, and concurrent assignments remain later choices.
5. Seek two participating experts but require one complete, authorized, sustainable service before building. One expert can support the learning pilot with an explicit supply limitation; do not claim expert choice or a resilient marketplace until a second independent supply path exists.
6. Provide recommendations and drafts. Customers carry out experiments and business changes. No automatic campaigns, external account actions, open publishing, public ratings, subscriptions, broad industries, or automatic knowledge approval in this release.

The user decides the category and licensing direction after Phase A evidence. Failure of a hypothesis is a reason to change or stop the corresponding investment, not to quietly redefine the original vision.

## 2. The service and complete participant experience

### Offer and input readiness

The candidate positioning service covers one product, one intended segment, one launch question, and one current positioning statement/page. Proposed pilot input limit: five selected company documents, up to twenty pages in total, plus one short company brief. These are workload assumptions to test in Phase B, then state in the listing. Do not accept extra documents silently or charge for a provider's own correction.

Before payment, the buyer confirms the decision, source audience, which sources are current, authorized approver, and useful decision date. The service requires at least product/offer facts and relevant customer evidence. The evidence analyst may flag gaps without inventing support. If missing evidence prevents the promised package, decline or rescope before charging. An evidence-gap assessment is a different offer requiring explicit agreement; it is not an undisclosed substitute.

The six-part decision package contains:

1. Current-positioning assessment and prioritized weaknesses.
2. An evidence map linking material factual conclusions to permitted sources; assumptions, inferences, and unknowns are distinguished.
3. Two defensible positioning directions, trade-offs, and a recommended choice. If evidence supports only one, explain that limitation and seek an agreed scope/remedy rather than manufacture a second direction.
4. A revised positioning statement and a draft launch message for the selected direction.
5. Three practical experiments with suggested owners, measures, and the decision each would inform. Measures may require the buyer to establish a baseline; invented targets are not evidence.
6. Unresolved disagreements, uncertainty, and next evidence needed.

Every agent-written conclusion is labeled “Agent-generated using [expert]'s authorized service guidance.” Genuine human review is separately attributed with the reviewer, reviewed edition/materials, observations, corrections, and date. The expert's contribution and the agents' application of it remain distinguishable even after review.

### Delivery acceptance and scope rules

Freeze the purchased offer, profile/guidance edition, inputs, audience, price, and deadlines with the order. Acceptance means the defined service was delivered, not that the recommendations guarantee business results.

The buyer and service owner check that all six promised parts are present; the work addresses the agreed company/question; every consequential factual claim has support or an explicit uncertainty label; major recommendations connect to company evidence and expert criteria; trade-offs and dissent are visible; experiments can inform a decision; and attribution/access terms are accurate. A critical claim without support blocks delivery until corrected or withdrawn.

- **Provider correction:** wrong source, unsupported claim presented as fact, missing promised section, failure to use agreed inputs, or inaccurate attribution. Correct without consuming the included preference revision. Repeated inability to meet scope triggers a remedy, not endless attempts or a new charge.
- **Included preference revision:** one consolidated buyer request to refine the original question using the agreed inputs, without a new product, segment, or substantial new research. A source correction that exposes provider error is handled as a correction, not automatically sold as new work.
- **New scope:** a distinct question, product, segment, or material new research. Explain the difference and obtain a fresh offer/approval before extra work. No forced repurchase to unlock a service correction.
- **Scope dispute:** the operator gives a written classification and remedy; a named backup handles disputed decisions or absence. Preserve the buyer's ability to report a material issue after acceptance.
- **Outcome disappointment:** record it as a usefulness signal and investigate service quality; do not promise business success or automatically treat every disagreement as service failure.

Proposed operating defaults to exercise in Phase B and freeze before sale: base delivery in three business days after confirmed payment and ready inputs; included revision in two business days after a consolidated request; human review in three business days after confirmed upgrade payment and the named expert's acceptance of the approved packet. Display the relevant time zone and start date before purchase. Never sell a deadline the expert/team has not accepted.

Buyer review window: five business days to accept, request the included revision, or raise an issue. Silence becomes “delivered, awaiting customer response,” not acceptance. Remind once; after ten business days of silence archive the inactive assignment while preserving access, the service-issue route, and the stated revision entitlement. The next assignment must not erase an unresolved issue.

An input request pauses the delivery clock visibly. After five business days without the required answer, the operator offers resume, agreed rescope, or cancellation/remedy and states the next date. Customer pause or stop halts future agent work promptly; it does not itself cancel confirmed payments or completed expert obligations. Show what has stopped, what remains owed, and applicable remedies. Do not keep a fixed-price customer paying for internal work overruns.

Proposed support standard: acknowledge service/payment/access exceptions within one business day; give an action, owner, and next update date within two. Escalate unresolved payment uncertainty immediately to the commerce owner. These are proposed commitments to validate against staffing before publication.

### Bounded agent collaboration

Four skills cover evidence assessment, positioning evaluation, critical review, and recommendation drafting. Each states its purpose, needed information, output, limitations, maintainer, and eligible agents. Members can discover these skills and assign approved skills to eligible agents within their responsibilities; presets limit arbitrary new capabilities, not this registry journey. Expert guidance shapes judgment without increasing authority.

Three agents have named human owners: evidence analyst, positioning lead, critical reviewer. The ordinary route is evidence handoff → recommendation → challenge → response/revision → synthesis. Each handoff carries the question, relevant source references, assumptions, permitted audience, responsible recipient, expected next result, and open issues.

Proposed limits: one initial challenge round, one response/revision round, and at most one member-requested alternative-view round within the purchased work allowance. Members can invite a permitted preset agent or already purchased service within the assignment's approved audience and scope. New paid expert participation requires separate owner approval, confirmed capacity, and payment; it cannot restart spending implicitly. Broader participants outside these bounds are explicitly out of pilot scope.

A useful challenge changes a recommendation, identifies consequential uncertainty, or is declined using case-specific evidence. Agreement is allowed; require no theatrical disagreement. Preserve a live disagreement when evidence is insufficient. Show a concise decision summary alongside the actual exchanges.

Stop further rounds when the allowance/round limit is reached, required evidence is absent, instructions conflict with access/scope, the buyer stops, or an owner must resolve a decision. Produce an honest partial status and a request for a human decision, not a falsely complete package. A limit reached because the provider underestimated promised work is a service problem to resolve at the agreed price.

### Human and operator journey

The founder creates the organization, invites a colleague, prepares company knowledge, selects the service, and approves the commitment. Members contribute and review within their access. The buyer understands both the service and profile boundary before paying. After discussion, the team reviews the package, obtains any included correction/revision, optionally buys available human review, and chooses whether to accept. A member can propose the result and business decision for Bonfire; an authorized owner separately approves trusted knowledge. Later the team records actions and experiment results or raises a distinct new paid question.

The expert contributes and approves guidance, listing, examples, allowed uses, maintenance, and compensation terms. For each actual review the expert accepts a particular approved packet and due date before a slot is sold; requests any additional context through the buyer's approval; submits personally attributed comments; completes one clarification round; closes access; and sees the compensation amount, due date, and payment status. Neither the agent nor operator signs comments in the expert's name.

The operator tracks readiness, guidance rights/editions, capacity, confirmed/pending money, waiting inputs, deadlines, corrections, scope disputes, review obligations, refund progress, and expert compensation. Every exception has one owner, a next action/date, and a customer-visible explanation. Operator access to private materials is explicitly limited and recorded. A backup can take over; customer trust must not depend on one unavailable staff member.

## 3. Owners, capacity, effort, and costs

These are provisional planning allowances for a new product, not quotes, proven delivery rates, or calendar promises. Re-estimate after Phase B and after the first complete paid workflow. Do not hide integration uncertainty under a small relative size.

Reference capacity: two full-time delivery contributors; a product lead at half time; a marketplace operator at half time; a design/research contributor at quarter time. Total: 3.25 full-time equivalents, at most about sixteen person-days per week before absence and coordination. Experts are additional, paid or explicitly recorded as subsidized. If fewer people are available, reduce concurrent work and extend elapsed time; do not assign the same person's days twice.

Name the people before starting each phase. One person can hold several roles only within their allocation:

- **Product lead:** one accountable decision owner for offer, research, funding gates, recruitment outcome, and release. Owns Phase A, Phase F, and Phase G.
- **Marketplace operator:** expert recruitment and rights, capacity, service queue, buyer support, disputes, compensation records; accountable for Phase E and backup coverage. Seeks a second decision-maker for contested remedies.
- **Workspace owner:** accountable for Bonfire fit, organizations, membership/access, and Phase C.
- **Agent experience owner:** accountable for NEAR AI fit, bounded roles, evidence quality, and Phase D. May be one delivery contributor with explicit allocation.
- **Commerce owner:** accountable for NEAR Intents fit, approval/payment/refund demonstrations, financial reconciliation, and customer/expert payment ownership. Can be the other delivery contributor; business responsibility must be accepted by a named seller/operator.
- **Evaluation owner:** accountable for Phase B's frozen comparisons and scoring records, normally the product lead with independent review support. A contributing expert is an adviser, not the sole judge of their own value.

Estimated team effort including product work and acceptance checks: Phase A 10–15 person-days; B 15–25; C 25–40; D 30–45; E 25–40; F 15–25; G 10–20. Total 130–210 person-days, with customer/expert waiting and follow-up additional. Phases A–B alone are 25–40 person-days: at the reference capacity, allow roughly three to five elapsed weeks including recruitment and demonstrations. After their gate, C–E are 80–125 person-days; allow roughly seven to eleven elapsed weeks including checks and coordination. A supported pilot may therefore begin around weeks ten to sixteen if recruitment and dependencies cooperate. Repeat-demand observation and release work can push the release decision to roughly months five to seven or later. These ranges require a dependency-based schedule after named capacity is known.

Initial expert allowance to validate: four to eight hours to prepare/authorize useful guidance, two to four hours for calibration, and one to two hours per actual review including clarification. Record actuals. Book review slots, not just general willingness. Research comparisons and independent scoring require separate consent, time, and compensation; they are not hidden included human review.

Before validation starts, set a cash cap and elapsed-time cap for A–B. Before full build, set a revised exposure cap for C–E. Before sale, freeze the price and fully costed order ceiling: agent work, expert rights/use and review payments, payment-related costs, readiness preparation, support, correction/revision, failed-order/refund costs, and ongoing guidance maintenance. Show one-time contribution/setup and experimental comparison costs separately with a conservative recovery assumption; do not treat free expert labor or unpaid staff as zero-cost delivery.

Proposed economic release gate: variable delivery costs across the entire paid cohort are no more than 80% of retained customer revenue, with no loss-making offer hidden by the other offer. The 20% buffer is a management hypothesis, not evidence of company profitability; show fixed operating and one-time costs separately. Check one additional failed/refunded order and one review overrun as downside cases. Freeze the threshold before observing paid results or choose a different explicit threshold with justification. A higher future price is not a passed gate until buyers actually pay it.

## 4. Compare value before attributing it

Use four real, consented briefs before the full build and repeat on four paid-pilot briefs. Reuse the same evaluation rules, not the same finished outputs. Do not use fictional successes to claim customer value.

For each brief, preserve the buyer's actual alternative, previous spend, and expected decision/time. Prepare three versions from the same frozen, permitted inputs and six-part scope:

1. **Ordinary alternative:** competent single-agent work with public/general guidance and a fair, reusable task brief. Do not deliberately weaken it or omit company evidence.
2. **Expert-guided single agent:** the same approach plus authorized expert guidance. This contrast estimates incremental expert-guidance value.
3. **Expert-guided collaboration:** the bounded three-agent process using that same guidance. This contrast estimates incremental collaboration value under that offer.

Use an equal maximum work allowance per version and record actual time/cost, including preparation. The ordinary alternative should include the cheap self-review a competent user would normally request within that allowance. Do not let one version see another's result. Freeze inputs before any version runs; buyer clarifications either go to all versions before scoring or start a separately identified new comparison. Keep operator editing, live expert input, and after-the-fact prompt tuning out of all scored versions. Record unavoidable assistance for each version and shared preparation separately.

In the paid-pilot set, at least two of the four briefs must be prepared by customers from the stated instructions without staff selecting evidence, rewriting the question, or editing the deliverable. Report their results separately; the expert and collaboration contrasts must each show at least one material improvement on this unaided subset before claiming independence from routine operator help. During B, try the same task to expose readiness problems early; assisted samples can guide changes but cannot prove unassisted readiness. If staff preparation is needed, preserve the customer's original packet and describe exactly what staff added and what it cost. Do not credit that preparation to expert guidance or agent collaboration. The final three-organization release rehearsal remains necessary.

Freeze a rubric before results: company relevance, evidence accuracy, decision/trade-off quality, actionable next steps, and serious unsupported claims. Score the first four dimensions from 1 to 5 with written anchors: 1 misses the decision or lacks usable support, 3 is usable with identified limitations, 5 clearly changes what the company can decide/do with appropriate support. A serious unsupported claim cannot be averaged away by fluent prose. Require a brief-specific explanation of a material change, not just a score preference.

Randomize and record display order, conceal production method and expert identity where practical, normalize headings/length allowance, and collect buyer and qualified evaluator scores independently before discussion. Prefer a scorer who did not author the guidance, produce the outputs, or earn a sales-linked payment; obtain company approval for what they see. An expert reviewing their own guidance can add commentary but does not supply independent confirmation. If an independent scorer cannot be recruited, disclose that limitation and do not claim independent validation.

Define a pairwise win as the buyer preferring one result for a stated decision-relevant reason and the qualified evaluator finding no material quality disadvantage or serious unsupported claim in it. Opposing rankings are mixed, not a win; ties remain ties. Keep every brief, refusal, exception, score, timing, and cost. Do not rerun only losses or exclude unflattering outcomes without reporting them.

Proposed learning gates on each four-brief set: expert-guided single-agent work wins over the ordinary alternative in at least three; collaboration wins over expert-guided single-agent work in at least three; each contrast has at least two specific material improvements. Each winning improvement must fit the predeclared delivery cost/time ceiling; buyers must explain why the improvement justifies the quoted service price and any extra wait over their existing alternative. These are investment thresholds with tiny samples, not statistical proof. If counts pass but buyer reasons are superficial, do not pass the value gate.

After scoring, deliver only an authorized, checked customer package. If real human review is purchased, preserve the pre-review result, record the expert's added changes, elapsed time, cost, and buyer assessment separately. Scoring by a research evaluator must not be passed off as the purchased expert's review or allowed to quietly improve a base-only delivery. Operator-edited samples are labeled assisted and excluded from claims about unassisted quality. Shared preparation may still help every version; this test cannot establish every interaction, general market superiority, or the value of operator help by itself.

## 5. Dependency evidence and early stop rules

Bonfire, NEAR AI, and NEAR Intents retain their PRD roles. No capability is assumed verified by this plan. The accountable owners maintain an evidence record: required customer outcome, authoritative source where needed, dated observation, constraints, unresolved gap, owner, affected promise, and go/change/stop decision. Documentation alone cannot establish the complete buyer experience.

- **Bonfire / workspace owner:** demonstrate organization and assignment access, relevant source identification, source updates/withdrawal, approval of trusted knowledge, and the proposed retention/sharing boundaries. If a critical confidentiality promise cannot be demonstrated, use fictional/nonconfidential material for learning and block confidential inputs and dependent release investment.
- **NEAR AI / agent experience owner:** demonstrate the intended roles can produce inspectable handoffs, reference evidence, stay within defined work/permission limits, stop/pause, and make work cost visible enough to price the offer. An inability to prove these outcomes blocks the affected product promise; do not assume the named service provides every control by itself.
- **NEAR Intents / commerce owner:** identify the intended buyer market, payment choice, payer prerequisites, full costs, recipient, confirmation evidence, uncertain/failed outcomes, and the workable refund/expert-compensation arrangement. Have an intended buyer unfamiliar with the route complete a small controlled purchase and recovery. Separate dislike of funding/payment steps from dislike of the service price. Do not claim supported currencies, settlement behavior, automatic refunds, or payouts without evidence.
- **Expert and seller / operator:** establish authorized contribution use, truthful listing claims, deliverable/internal-use rights, actual review capacity, compensation, seller responsibility, remedies, and support coverage. Check obligations specific to the chosen commercial arrangement with appropriate advisers when necessary; do not turn discovery into a broad legal project.

Resolve critical dependency outcomes during A–B before full dependent build. Reversible, isolated investigations can continue while a gap is owned, but “owned gap” is not a passed gate. Use a controlled small payment/refund in B to validate feasibility and repeat the complete product journey in E. If any required route fails, propose an explicit change with user approval or stop that path. Do not quietly replace NEAR Intents, NEAR AI, or Bonfire. A manual service or alternative payment may be discussed as a separate experiment, never called the promised release.

## 6. Purchasing, commitments, cancellation, and expert payments

Before accepting money, identify the seller, who receives funds, who decides remedies, who executes/finances refunds, and who owes each expert. If one business performs several roles, name it. Expert payment is not assumed to be automatic through NEAR Intents or contingent on the buyer clicking accept.

The buyer sees service, scope, guidance edition, inputs, price and payment-related costs, approver, deadline/capacity, cancellation/remedy rules, remaining budget, and profile boundary before approval. Freeze the commitment in an agreed accounting currency with an approved all-in amount. Explain any payment conversion/return-cost exposure explicitly; if the verified route cannot honor the promised total or remedy, reprice/rescope with fresh approval or do not sell. Any changed terms or amount require fresh approval. Set an approval/quote expiry and show it.

Only the owner approves purchases in the pilot. Members and agents can request them. An agent's allowance does not expand the organization budget. No separate buyer usage charge is added for included agent work. A human-review upgrade has separate capacity, approved sharing, price, and approval.

Keep approval, money, and work states distinct:

- **Requested / awaiting approval:** no paid work; show the proposed commitment, no implication of payment.
- **Approved / payment not yet initiated:** hold budget and the stated capacity until displayed expiry; release only if there is no possible initiated payment.
- **Payment pending or outcome uncertain:** retain the commitment and investigate; do not start paid work, invite another payment, or release the hold just because the buyer left or a display timed out.
- **Confirmed payment:** convert the hold to spending once and start the matching assignment once after inputs are ready. Returning to the page or retrying a button cannot create another purchase or debit.
- **Definitively failed/expired without possible settlement:** release the hold and unsold capacity; permit an explicit new attempt. Explain evidence that the prior attempt cannot still complete.
- **Cancellation requested:** stop future work according to the service policy, but keep the money obligation visible. A pending payment is not made failed by a cancellation request.
- **Late confirmation after cancellation, expiry, or changed approval:** do not restart work automatically or silently create a second commitment. Commerce owner reconciles the money; offer an authorized continuation only if terms/capacity still work, otherwise the promised refund/remedy. Preserve enough reserved exposure to cover this case.
- **Refund requested / approved / in progress / completed / failed:** display these separately and show amount, responsible party, next update, and evidence of completion. Do not label requested money “refunded.” For partial refunds show both retained and returned amounts. Keep original spending and refund history; restore usable budget only according to the stated confirmed-refund convention.

Proposed remedy policy to validate and publish before sale: full return of the promised customer charge if the service cannot start or the provider cannot deliver agreed scope and no alternative is accepted. After work starts, voluntary cancellation may retain only the disclosed, agreed earned portion; the buyer sees this rule before paying. A missed human review gives a choice of an approved new date or refund of the undelivered upgrade. Full/partial remedy for defective delivery depends on what can be corrected and accepted, with an escalation owner; a service cannot keep the full price for an undelivered promise merely because an expert has been paid. The seller must fund the promised remedy even when payment costs or compensation are unrecoverable. Proposed default: initiate an approved refund within two business days, publish the verified method-specific expected completion time, and show the next update date while unresolved. Do not promise an unverified return time. If the chosen payment route makes these promises infeasible, change the offer explicitly before sale.

Agree expert preparation/maintenance compensation, per-use payment if any, actual-review fee, earned/cancelled-work rules, due dates, and who bears buyer refunds. Maintain expert obligations separately from customer payment status. Reserve resources for confirmed refunds and earned expert compensation; don't treat pending commitments as spendable revenue. No sale is permitted against unavailable review capacity or an unfunded promised remedy.

Commerce acceptance exercises: owner approval; member/agent bypass attempt; budget exceeded; simultaneous purchase/review requests; repeated/abandoned attempt; uncertain payment; late confirmation after cancellation; approval expiry or loss of approver authority; confirmed charge with interrupted work; full and partial refund; pending/failed refund; and expert payment despite a customer dispute. Practice destructive/rare cases safely; validate the essential real purchase/return path with real funds at small scale. At each step identify what the customer sees, money held/owed, available budget, capacity, owner, and next action. Every amount and promised expert payment must reconcile before closing the pilot.

## 7. Company knowledge, audience, and source changes

Begin with owner/member responsibilities, not elaborate custom roles. Owners manage membership, spending, and trusted-knowledge approval. Members contribute, request work, and review within granted access. Keep at least one owner; reassign agent and open-assignment responsibility before an owner leaves. Removing a member closes access to sources, work, and purchasing; existing commitments go to a remaining authorized owner for reconciliation rather than disappearing.

For each source identify organization, origin, permitted audience, source owner, current/superseded status, and assignment use. Before work, decide who can see the discussion and deliverable. Use only material permitted for that audience or explicitly restrict the assignment; an agent's broader access must not widen what members, experts, evaluators, or operators can see. Derived summaries can disclose confidential facts just as originals can.

Before actual expert review, show the buyer the specific output and supporting material to be shared, reviewer identity, purpose, access duration, and retention limits. The organization owner plus the relevant source authority approves sharing; one approver cannot grant material they have no right to share. The expert sees no unrelated organization material. More context requires another sharing decision, not automatic Bonfire access. Research evaluation and operator troubleshooting have separate purpose-limited approval and recorded access.

Freeze the source editions used for a run. When a source changes, mark it changed; do not silently rewrite the historical evidence trail. If a material correction or access withdrawal affects active work, pause the affected work/sharing, identify recommendations needing reconsideration, and ask an authorized member to approve a revised input set or remedy. Superseded historical deliverables are labeled with the affected sources and review status. A withdrawn source cannot remain an excuse for unqualified current advice.

Agree retention/removal behavior before accepting private information, including existing outputs and material already approved for external review. Revoke future access promptly; identify downstream copies and any remaining retention obligations. Do not promise retrieval of an already downloaded copy unless it can actually be established. Company inputs and derived outputs cannot become profile examples or marketplace material without separate explicit permission. Agent output becomes trusted Bonfire knowledge only after human approval.

Acceptance checks cover two organizations; different member permissions inside one organization; restricted sources combined into a shared summary; agent owner removal; last-owner continuity; source edit/supersession/withdrawal mid-run; an expert asking for more context; operator/evaluator access; and attempted instructions in a source that would alter permissions or spending. An actual disclosure triggers containment, affected-customer communication, corrective action, and recurrence checks before further use. Do not record a severe incident as erased because access has since been closed.

## 8. Investment, pilot, and release decisions

Freeze the applicable gates, cohort definitions, price, cost limits, and dates before observing outcomes. A failed gate does not authorize expansion through a hopeful narrative. All thresholds below are proposed management choices to confirm in Phase A; they are not market facts or statistically validated sample sizes.

**After A–B — fund/change/stop the full build.** Require an authorized useful contribution, three qualified priced pilot commitments, the four-brief comparison evidence, critical dependency demonstrations, explicit service terms, named capacity, and an affordable next-stage exposure cap. A priced commitment is recruitment evidence, not a paid sale. Expert-value failure changes the offer/expert; collaboration failure changes the handoffs/roles. Allow one bounded four-brief rerun with a stated change and cost cap. Persistent failure stops the proposed offer or requires an explicit user-approved direction change. Collaboration remains a product priority; do not silently replace it with a single-agent marketplace because the comparison failed.

**Before paid pilot — safety and delivery gate.** Critical rights, privacy, permission, approval, payment, refund funding, attribution, and deliverability checks must pass. Actual review requires a committed reviewer/packet/slot. A failed critical check blocks the affected sale or private-material use regardless of buyer enthusiasm. Run comparisons with nonconfidential material while resolving privacy gaps if useful.

**After paid pilot — release/change/hold/stop.** Six enrolled organizations receive the frozen offer; retain them in the denominator even if they decline, cannot pay, or drop out. Require:

- At least four full-price, completed, nonrefunded base purchases retained at the readout cutoff, at least ten business days after delivery and with no unresolved material service issue. Discounted, partially refunded, or purchase-incentivized orders do not satisfy this gate. Report warm relationships/referrals separately; a relationship alone does not invalidate a genuine full-price order. Later refunds update the retained-demand record rather than disappear after the cutoff.
- At least four accepted packages within the included preference revision, with corrections separately counted; at least three buyers report a concrete action and identify it. No undelivered service or serious unsupported claim is counted as accepted quality merely because the buyer clicked a button.
- Four paired three-version comparisons meeting the §4 expert and collaboration gates, actual time/cost limits, and disclosed assistance rules. Report ties, disagreements, adverse effects, and rater conflicts.
- Two genuine separately paid human reviews if the upgrade is to launch, with both buyers able to identify the person's contribution, assess it at the stated price, and receive the promised scope. If not achieved, review demand is unproven; omit the purchasable upgrade until validated rather than imply it passed.
- Observed delivery costs meeting the frozen economic gate at prices actually retained, with support, corrections, expert payments, and failed orders included. A hypothetical higher price or untested scope cut means change/rerun, not pass.
- No unresolved critical incident, unaccounted customer payment/refund, or overdue obligation; preserve serious incident history and demonstrate the preventive fix before further orders. Every refund and expert obligation is reconciled or explicitly scheduled and funded, with no overdue earned expert payment.
- At least two of the original six organizations make a distinct second full-price purchase in the observation window. It must not be an included correction/revision, a split first assignment, or a subsidized purchase presented as organic repeat demand.

Observe repeat demand for sixty days after each first accepted delivery; allow one extension to ninety days only where a genuine next decision was identified before results. At the final date, no eligible decision means recurrence is unproven, not passed. If fewer than two return, change the offer/cadence with evidence or stop expansion. A safe, clearly supported, capacity-limited experiment may remain open under a new bounded research decision, but does not become the public release or proven repeat-demand marketplace by default.

**Unassisted release gate.** After the pilot gates, three new organizations complete the core journey without staff taking over setup, evidence preparation, purchase, discussion controls, revision, or acceptance. Include two members together, a person switching organizations, and both base-only and validated actual-review journeys across the group. Genuine expert review and disclosed exception handling are legitimate service work; routine hidden operator preparation or editing fails this gate. Recheck critical exceptions after fixes. Set order limits and support coverage before opening access.

**Expansion gate.** Only after repeat purchases, continuing expert supply, safe unassisted operation, and demonstrated economics should the team consider another category, larger councils, broader licensing, or subscriptions. A small successful pilot authorizes a limited service, not broad claims about councils, experts, or business impact.

## 9. Phases, tasks, and acceptance evidence

Each phase ends with one accountable owner's written go/change/stop decision and linked evidence. Work may overlap only when dependencies are independently met and named capacity allows it. These are work phases, not instructions to create documents or hire a separate person for every role.

### Phase A — Choose a reachable problem and an honest offer

**Accountable:** product lead. **Effort:** 10–15 team person-days plus expert preparation; recruitment capped initially at three elapsed weeks. **Dependency:** user selects trial direction and validation exposure limits; name owners.

Tasks:

1. Establish capacity, cash/time caps, target buyer market, seller responsibility, and intended payment constraints. Keep the category and profile boundary explicitly proposed until reviewed.
2. Start with a planning funnel of twenty targeted organizations, aiming for ten qualified conversations and six eventual pilot participants. These are recruitment effort assumptions, not conversion forecasts. Log contact source, warm relationship, qualifying decision/date, budget authority, current alternative/spend, evidence readiness, payment familiarity, and refusal reason. Aim for at least two eventual buyers without close personal ties; disclose if unreachable.
3. Ask about both the first problem and the next distinct decision. Do not recruit for an artificially recurring problem. Test service-bound profile comprehension and demand for reuse; record lost demand rather than assume the boundary is harmless.
4. Seek two experts, with one as the complete initial supply path and the second's outcome recorded. Agree contribution/example rights, expertise, actual original criteria, prohibited/confidential inputs, usage boundary, truthful attribution, maintenance, withdrawal/existing-order treatment, compensation, and capacity. Authorization is required for paid named-expert use; independently inspired profiles stay out of launch.
5. Propose the exact scope, prices, discount policy, quality rubric, correction/revision rules, deadlines, retention/sharing boundaries, and remedy policy. Obtain at least three explicit commitments at the proposed pilot price from qualified decision-makers; record that no money has yet proved demand.
6. Begin direct dependency evidence gathering with the owners in §5. Establish any market-specific advice actually needed for the chosen selling arrangement.

**Acceptance:** a written offer buyers can explain accurately; one authorized contribution pack and booked rehearsal participation; three qualified priced commitments; a dated recruitment and dependency log; initial unit-cost assumptions; explicit user category/licensing choice and named capacity.

**Decision:** proceed to B within the validation cap. If recruitment, expert rights/incentives, or buyer reuse expectations conflict with the offer, choose a better-supported narrow offer or stop. One short recruitment extension requires a reason and new cap; silence is not evidence. Full build remains blocked.

### Phase B — Prove service value and dependency feasibility cheaply

**Accountable:** evaluation owner. **Effort:** 15–25 team person-days plus calibration/scoring and external waiting. **Dependencies:** A's authorized guidance, real consented briefs, intended buyer route, agreed validation cap. No product build required for manually prepared research samples.

Tasks:

1. Use four real candidate briefs and the three-version comparison in §4. A contributing expert checks guidance fidelity; qualified independent scoring checks usefulness without knowing production method where practical. Record manual preparation and expert input.
2. Exercise the proposed input limit, six-part package, work-round limit, missing evidence, useful disagreement, member challenge, and provider-correction versus preference-revision classification. Measure time/cost, then adjust and freeze the offer before taking paid orders.
3. Have a representative buyer inspect and explain price, deliverable, license boundary, real expert involvement, revision/remedy, and next steps without operator persuasion. Test why they would pay instead of using their existing alternative.
4. Demonstrate the critical Bonfire and NEAR AI product outcomes with permitted material. Resolve essential gaps; not every vendor feature must be native if the promised outcome is demonstrably attainable, but do not change required vendor roles quietly.
5. Commerce owner completes the controlled intended-buyer purchase, known confirmation, interruption/uncertainty recovery, return-of-funds path, and seller/expert-payment responsibility check. Document prerequisites, full costs, friction, and limits. Rehearse rare adverse cases safely rather than claim they were all observed live.
6. Have the actual expert accept an approved sample packet, ask a bounded clarification, submit attributed comments, close access, and confirm compensation/continuation terms. Use a fictional packet if confidential-sharing controls are not yet demonstrated.
7. Re-estimate build effort and order economics from observed work. Set the C–E cash/time exposure cap and explicit launch cost target; schedule named people and expert slots.

**Acceptance:** §4's comparison gates or an explicit bounded change decision; no critical unresolved dependency for dependent build; demonstrated buyer purchase/remedy feasibility; one complete expert rehearsal; offer/cost/quality records and buyer comprehension. This establishes a reason to invest, not self-service readiness or statistical proof.

**Decision:** fund the narrowed build only if the §8 pre-build gate passes. Allow at most one justified rerun of four briefs. If an essential vendor/rights/payment outcome fails, stop that path and bring a concrete change to the user. Do not spend through the full build hoping to settle the business case later.

### Phase C — Establish the shared workspace and trusted evidence

**Accountable:** workspace owner. **Effort:** 25–40 team person-days. **Dependencies:** B's full-build decision, demonstrated Bonfire suitability, approved access/retention policy, named delivery capacity.

Tasks:

1. Complete organization creation, business goal, invitations/removal, owner/member responsibilities, last-owner protection, switching, and active-organization visibility across work and purchases.
2. Let permitted members add/identify bounded company material, set audience/use, distinguish current/superseded sources, and prepare the agreed ready brief. Reassign agents and work when a responsible person leaves.
3. Complete assignment audience, derived-output, source-correction/withdrawal, retained-output, support-access, and human knowledge-approval rules from §7.
4. Introduce budgets, owner approval, and labeled practice purchases. Keep the skill registry and agents within organization boundaries.

**Acceptance:** an owner and colleague independently prepare a ready brief; a person switches between two organizations; restricted members and agents cannot widen source/output access; removing a member closes access; source changes lead to the stated response; unapproved output does not become trusted knowledge. Exercise every applicable §7 case.

**Decision:** proceed to D only with safe shared inputs. Fix critical access gaps before confidential customer material; other independent validation may continue. Re-estimate if permission or source-change work exceeds assumptions.

### Phase D — Make agent communication consequential and controllable

**Accountable:** agent experience owner. **Effort:** 30–45 team person-days. **Dependencies:** C's ready evidence journey; B's NEAR AI fit and collaboration decision; authorized guidance edition.

Tasks:

1. Publish four skill descriptions and three agent profiles with human owners, purpose, guidance, permissions, allowances, and limits. Let permitted members discover and assign the approved skills to eligible agents without gaining new access or spending authority.
2. Complete the bounded discussion and evidence-bearing handoffs, member-requested alternative, approved invitation, challenge response, dissent, and concise synthesis from §2.
3. Deliver visible progress, questions, pause/resume/stop, round/work limits, and clear incomplete-result/service-remedy behavior. Member redirection remains inside approved scope; added spending requires approval.
4. Exercise conflicting sources, thin evidence, a known misleading claim, an unwarranted challenge, permission-changing instructions inside a source, a source withdrawal, work cap, and owner removal.

**Acceptance:** materially important claims trace to permitted evidence or uncertainty; a seeded substantive weakness is caught; a justified disagreement remains visible; a supported conclusion survives an unsupported challenge; human controls halt new work; limits create a clear next decision without an added usage bill. Actual exchanges and their effect are inspectable; ordinary agreement is not penalized.

**Decision:** proceed to E when the promised package and controls can be demonstrated. If collaboration adds only noise or still fails B's value test, change roles/hand-offs and rerun the affected comparison before selling a collaboration advantage. No invented consensus or theatrical dissent to pass a checklist.

### Phase E — Complete sale, delivery, actual review, and exception handling

**Accountable:** marketplace operator. **Effort:** 25–40 team person-days. **Dependencies:** C–D accepted, commerce owner available, B's payment evidence, funded remedy responsibility, expert rights and accepted review slots.

Tasks:

1. Publish the curated catalog by problem, expert, price, and human involvement, with industry/category, example, suitability, limits, scope, edition, deadlines, all-in costs, license boundary, revision/correction/remedy policy. One listing is honest where only one supply path exists; do not invent expert choice.
2. Complete the owner approval, pending commitment, intended NEAR Intents payment journey, receipts, budgets, and exception cases in §6. Rehearse the real product purchase and return of funds at low value before ordinary customer charging.
3. Complete delivery, commenting, provider corrections, included preference revision, scope dispute, acceptance/silence, pause/stop, and separate Bonfire proposal/approval. Agree all unresolved clocks and amounts before sale.
4. Complete the expert's accepted packet/slot, buyer sharing approval, additional-context request, attributed comments, clarification, access closure, and compensation record. Rehearse a declined or late review and buyer-approved reschedule/refund; do not substitute a reviewer silently.
5. Give the operator and backup a manageable queue and exception duties from §2. Agree a weekly order cap no higher than the lesser of base-delivery capacity and booked review capacity for review orders. Calculate cap from measured work, available hours, deadlines, and a support buffer.
6. Rehearse a full assignment from organization setup to money reconciliation and accepted/trusted output, including a revision, actual expert review, scope dispute, source change, and payment/delivery exception. Practice cases need not be forced into one confusing customer order.

**Acceptance:** the seller can demonstrate every §6 money case and §7 sharing boundary; customer and expert can each complete their promised journey; no service is sold without capacity; all payment/refund/expert responsibilities have a named owner, amount, next action, and funding. A representative customer explains what is bought and who actually reviews it.

**Decision:** product lead authorizes only a clearly supported, capacity-limited paid pilot after §8's safety/delivery gate. Any critical rights, access, money, or attribution failure blocks the affected sale. A review gap can disable that upgrade while base-service learning proceeds safely; it cannot count as validation of review demand.

### Phase F — Run the paid pilot and observe real return demand

**Accountable:** product lead. **Effort:** 15–25 team person-days of recruitment, measurement, and coordination, plus separately costed delivery/expert work; up to six weeks to recruit/run the cohort and sixty days of follow-up, with only the stated ninety-day extension. **Dependencies:** E's safety gate, frozen offer/prices/gates, support coverage and capacity.

Tasks:

1. Enroll six organizations with real decisions; at least three involve two human members. Seek both experts and at least two paid actual reviews where sustainable capacity exists. Keep backups for scheduling, but do not replace negative outcomes in the decision denominator. If six cannot enroll within the window, report the funnel and change recruitment/offer or stop the intended six-organization test.
2. Present the frozen price; record discounts, relationships, payment friction, refusals, cancellations, refunds, and assistance. Free work, a priced commitment, or a refundable reservation is not a retained paid order. Keep the six-organization cohort denominator and the wider acquisition funnel.
3. Run four consented three-version comparisons using §4's frozen method. Score before any operator editing or genuine expert review, account for all costs, and preserve mixed findings.
4. Measure readiness/support time, delivery, corrections/revisions, acceptance, source quality, useful challenges, unsupported claims, action taken, review contribution, and actual order economics. Separate base-only, reviewed, assisted, and research-comparison results.
5. Seek a distinct second full-price order when the buyer has a real next decision. Follow up on experiment results and whether the original recommendation helped; do not claim a business outcome caused by the service from these reports alone.
6. Write a readout against every §8 gate, with raw counts/denominators, retained prices, time, costs, incidents, buyer/expert reasons, recruitment limitations, and the next explicit decision.

**Acceptance:** complete readout and reconciled money/expert obligations, whether results pass or fail. At least one expert confirms willingness to continue at observed effort and agreed compensation; record the second expert's actual supply outcome. Report the repeat result on the fixed dates, not an open-ended “still waiting.”

**Decision:** release preparation only if the pilot gates pass. For a failed price/scope or workflow gate, one bounded change/rerun must retest the changed claim at an actual price and preserve prior negatives. Critical failure stops new affected orders until fixed and rechecked. Unproven repeat demand permits only an expressly bounded supported experiment, not expansion. Persistent lack of expert or collaboration value stops that proposition or requires a user-approved direction change.

### Phase G — Demonstrate the narrow service without routine assistance

**Accountable:** product lead. **Effort:** 10–20 team person-days plus legitimate delivery/expert work and customer waiting. **Dependencies:** F's passed gates, preventive fixes verified, continuing expert terms, ready support/commerce coverage.

Tasks:

1. Remove the routine staff preparation/takeover observed in the pilot. Do not hide it as “curation.” Keep legitimate expert work and a disclosed exception service.
2. Have three new organizations complete setup, sources, purchase, discussion, review/revision, acceptance, and knowledge proposal/approval themselves. Include multiple members, organization switching, and base-only plus validated actual-review journeys across the group. Measure every intervention and classify it honestly.
3. Recheck access/source withdrawal, owner removal, challenge/stop/limits, uncertain/duplicate payment, failed/pending refund, missed review, and compensation after changes. Preserve severe incident history and demonstrate recurrence prevention.
4. Set the published order cap, capacity/backups, support deadlines, remedies, price, attribution, and guidance rights/editions. Restrict orders when delivery or review capacity is exhausted.
5. Start a monthly product decision review of repeat full-price use, acquisition/refusal reasons, acceptance/corrections/refunds, actionable recommendations, expert retention, collaboration benefit, assistance, and actual economics.

**Acceptance:** all three new organizations finish the promised core journey without staff operating it for them; any exception has disclosed owner and remedy; no critical issue is open; all applicable PRD first-release requirements are demonstrated; the product lead and operator sign off with commerce/access owners' evidence.

**Decision:** limited public release of the validated narrow service within the capacity cap. Keep a human-review upgrade unavailable if its own gate did not pass. No broad catalog, standalone profile promise, autonomous paid participants, or new category is authorized merely by this launch.

## 10. Remaining user decisions and residual risks

The next user decisions are limited to four:

1. Trial customer/problem and whether profile use may be limited to the purchased service while reusable-profile demand is tested.
2. Available buyer/expert introductions, or a bounded mandate for recruitment if there are none.
3. Named team capacity, maximum validation cash/time exposure, and who can authorize the later build cap.
4. Intended buyer market/payment constraints and the business/person accepting seller, refund, and expert-payment responsibility.

Within those decisions, the product lead and operator propose prices, exact clocks/input limits, review terms, and operational details from evidence, then freeze them before taking money. Defaults in this plan are testable proposals, not agreed customer promises. Material changes to the PRD's vendor roles, central collaboration, marketplace direction, or access/spending promises return to the user explicitly.

Residual risks remain: four comparisons and six buyers are a small selected sample; methods may be guessed despite blinding; expert guidance and collaboration may help only some tasks; positioning may not recur; buyer payment friction may outweigh service value; and expert availability, dependency behavior, support load, and effort estimates may change. Internal delivery costs do not establish buyer willingness to pay, and positive pilot contribution does not establish overall profitability.

This plan reduces avoidable investment and launch ambiguity. It does not establish vendor capabilities, customer demand, broad superiority, or guaranteed success. The next authorized work should be bounded validation, followed by an evidence-based funding decision.
